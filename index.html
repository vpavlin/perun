Perun is a private GPS tracker for running, hiking and cycling. It runs on Android and on Basecamp (desktop), and the two sync directly over Logos: no account and no server of ours in between.

What it does

Record the run. Keep the run.

Live map, splits, pace and elevation: the detail you want from a tracker, without handing your movements to anyone.

Run, hike & ride

Run, trail, walk, hike, ride and MTB. Live map, per-km splits, pace and elevation, with auto-pause at red lights. Recording continues with the screen off. A rolling median filter removes GPS altitude spikes, so elevation gain is believable.

Annotate the route

Pin a text note, photo or voice note to a point on the run, then edit or delete it later. Annotations sync to Basecamp, where you can see the photos and play the voice notes.

Replay & video

Scrub a replay along the route, or export an MP4 replay video (hardware H.264) in 1:1, 16:9 or 9:16. Your photos and voice notes appear in it. It is rendered on the phone, with no upload.

GPX & image export

Export any run as standard GPX (with its sport type) for Strava, Garmin or your own archive. You can also share a summary image, and hide the map first if you don't want to reveal where you were.

Analytics on the desktop

Perun Analytics on Basecamp gives a bigger screen for every synced run: splits, elevation, route map, annotations and replay, plus trends, weekly distance and personal bests. You can export GPX or save an image from there too.

Sync over Logos

By default Perun uses the phone's shared Loam node, which serves all your Logos apps. Without Loam, it runs its own node. Recording never needs a network; you sync when you're ready.

How it works · privacy model

Pairing is the whole key.

There's no account. Your phone and your Basecamp share one secret, and everything Perun sends is sealed with keys derived from it.

Pair once

  1. In Basecamp, open Perun Analytics and choose Pair phone. It shows a QR code and a perun://pair link.
  2. In the app, open pairing and scan the QR, or paste the link.
  3. Both screens show the same 3-word fingerprint. If the words match, you are paired with the right device.
prefermicrowaveskullcap
Example fingerprint. Yours will be different.

What is sealed, and who sees what

  • Keys come from the pair secret. The 256-bit secret travels only in the QR or link. Both devices derive the encryption key, the Logos topic and the fingerprint from it (HKDF-SHA256).
  • Every message is sealed (runs, annotations, sync requests) with ChaCha20-Poly1305, bound to the topic.
  • Relay and store nodes on the Logos network carry only ciphertext on a random-looking topic. They can see that a message passed, its size and its timing, but not the route or who you are.
  • Photos and voice notes are sealed on the phone and uploaded to an attachment server you set (Perun Core on your desktop runs one). The server only holds ciphertext. Text notes need no server.
  • Map tiles for the in-app map and the video basemaps come from OpenStreetMap and Esri ArcGIS, so those services see the map area you view.

The myth
What is yours should stay yours — and what comes for it should be met with thunder.

Perun is the Slavic god of thunder and the storm — sky-father, lord of the sacred oak, keeper of order. He rides the heavens in a chariot of fire, and in his hand is the axe of lightning that always returns to him once thrown.

His eternal adversary is Veles, the horned serpent who lives among the roots of the world. Veles slips up from the dark to steal — cattle, waters, people — and drag them down to hoard where no light reaches. Each time, Perun hunts him across the sky and strikes, splitting oak and stone, until what was taken is returned. Then the rain falls, and the world is set right.

It is an old story about a simple idea, and it stands guard over the Dark Forest — the deep, unmapped place where a thing can stay hidden and safe.

Today the serpent wears a different face. Modern trackers take your movements, your routes, your home, and hoard them in databases you will never see. Perun takes the old bargain back. Your runs stay on your own devices, sealed with a key only they hold.

Install

Two halves, one private whole.

The phone records the runs and the desktop analyses them. They find each other over Logos.

  2. Install Perun. Updates arrive through F-Droid.
  3. Loam recommended: install Loam from the same repo and approve Perun once. Then all your Logos apps share one node. Without Loam, Perun runs its own node.

Status & limitations

Honest about the edges.

  • The desktop is for analytics only. You record on the phone. Basecamp receives, stores and analyses the runs.
  • Run sync is something you trigger. Tap Sync to Basecamp on a run. Basecamp should be running at the time, or you can run your own always-on perun_core hub (optional). There is no public hub.
  • Photos and voice notes need an attachment server the phone can reach. Text notes sync without one.
  • Map tiles need a network. The basemap is fetched from OpenStreetMap or Esri. Recording and the no-basemap video work offline.
  • Android is arm64 only. Basecamp 0.2.x on Linux x86_64. Basecamp 0.3 support is coming.
  • Bluetooth sync through Loam is experimental and Android only.
